# LGN Opportunity — Meeting Notes & Reflection

*March 2026*

---

## The Meeting

Dr Mohammed Bahja — second marker for my MSc thesis, Lecturer in Computer Science at University of Birmingham — invited me into a conversation that went well beyond academic supervision. He recognised my technical ability through my FinRisk thesis and flagged a concrete opportunity: joining his venture as a technical cofounder, with 5% equity, working roughly 2 hours a day toward a demo targeting Gulf enterprise clients.

The demo was originally scheduled during Ramadan 2026. A regional conflict extended the timeline by a month or two, putting the window around May–June 2026 — which coincidentally aligns with my MSc submission date.

---

## About London Gulf Nexus (LGN)

**Website:** https://londongulfnexus.com

London Gulf Nexus is a consortium positioning itself at the intersection of UK academic credibility and Gulf enterprise AI adoption. It has offices in both the UAE and the UK.

**What they do:**

Their business operates across three pillars:

**1. AI Training & Upskilling**
Their flagship methodology is *UPGRADEjourney™*, a digital upskilling programme aimed at breaking psychological barriers to technology adoption. They offer sector-specific AI courses targeting legal professionals, banking and FinTech, real estate, and custom enterprise programmes. They also run an AI Kids Club. Their client base spans Oil & Gas, Finance, Manufacturing, Healthcare, and Government & Military sectors. They claim 600k+ clients and partnerships including Google.

**2. AI Solutions**
Their product suite includes:

- *Proactive Learning Assistant* — the RAG-based teaching assistant that's the core of this opportunity
- *Legal Documents Analyzer* — document intelligence for legal use cases
- *AI in a Box* — packaged AI deployment for enterprises
- Automation, AI Bots, and AI Agents

**3. Consultancy**
They offer NEXUS consultancy services and ISO/IEC 42001 certification support — notably the world's first certifiable standard for responsible AI management. This ISO angle is strategically smart for Gulf enterprise clients who need governance credibility.

**Academic Credibility Layer:**
LGN claims a consortium of 50+ elite professors from top UK universities. Dr Bahja's own profile supports this — he's a lecturer at University of Birmingham with consultancy relationships with NHS, Dubai Police, and Aramco Saudi, and prior EU research projects (PolicyCompass, GreenDC).

**What the business actually is:**
The moat is relationships and credibility, not technical innovation. Gulf enterprises are early in their AI adoption curve and will pay well for packaged solutions delivered by people they trust. LGN is deliberately positioned to exploit that dynamic — UK academic prestige plus Gulf network equals enterprise deals. The technical work is the delivery mechanism, not the differentiation.

## The Opportunity

**The pitch:** Join as a technical contributor (framed as potential cofounder) on the Proactive Learning Assistant — a RAG system for enterprise learning and knowledge management. 5% equity. ~2 hours/day commitment alongside current Access Group role.

**Three demo clients:**

- Saudi Aramco
- Academy of Learning (Saudi)
- Khalifa University, Abu Dhabi

These are not small logos. Aramco is one of the most valuable companies in the world. Khalifa University is a well-funded Abu Dhabi research institution. Landing even one of these as a paying client creates credibility that opens the entire Gulf market.

**The team:**
5 existing technical contributors from Oxford and other top universities. Strong research and ML depth. Notable gap: product sense. No one currently bridges technical capability and client-facing product thinking.

**What he said explicitly:** He valued my ability to bridge product and AI. He mentioned that if I showed leadership qualities I could go up fast. He chose equity over salary deliberately — he could pay UK contractor rates easily, but he wants owners, not employees.

---

## Why This Is Actually Interesting

**The role is rare.** The gap in that team isn't another ML engineer. It's someone who can look at what they've built and ask: what does the Aramco stakeholder need to feel in the first 10 minutes? What's missing that will make them say no in the boardroom even if the demo works technically? That's not a research question — it's a product and trust question.

My background makes this specific role unusual to find: 8 years shipping products to real users, healthcare experience where clinical adoption failed until the interface matched existing workflows, FinRisk thesis measuring trust in AI outputs at the HITL layer, and an MSc giving me the language to work with the research team directly.

**The timing aligns.** The demo window of May–June 2026 is when my thesis submits. Walking out of my MSc with a live enterprise demo to Aramco in the portfolio is a meaningfully different position than just "completed thesis."

**The financial arbitrage is real but not the point.** UK contractor rates at my level would be £500–700/day. He chose not to use that as the hook. That's a deliberate signal — he wants a different kind of relationship than money buys. The ownership dynamic produces different behaviour than employment.

**This is an access layer, not the destination.** Gulf enterprise AI conversations that would take years to enter independently become accessible through his network. The firsthand exposure to what governance and adoption problems look like from the client side is research I can't do from Malaysia or Birmingham alone.

---

## Key Observations from the Meeting

**His framing of the gap was "features."** When asked what was missing in the product, his answer was features. That single word reveals a lot. Researchers default to additive thinking — what else do we build? Product thinking is different: what does the client need to feel, and what's in the way of that? The gap isn't features. It's probably that what they've built doesn't tell a coherent story to a non-technical stakeholder.

**Observability is an obvious gap.** Enterprise clients — especially Aramco — operate in environments where accountability and traceability are non-negotiable. A teaching assistant that answers questions is a demo. A teaching assistant with a complete audit trail that compliance teams can review, managers can monitor, and leadership can report on — that's a governance product. My FinRisk work already solved this at the HITL layer (traversal paths, provenance flags, per-stage timestamps, edit preservation). This is directly transferable.

**A corporate engineer would be a different kind of mismatch.** Not wrong technically, but they'd arrive solving engineering problems (scalability, architecture, CI/CD) while the actual gap is product problems. The research team would also struggle to integrate that profile — different definitions of "done," different intuitions about what matters.

**He's thinking about the right hire.** The fact that he framed the role as "bridging product and AI" rather than "additional engineer" suggests he has a clear enough sense of the gap to name it, even if he can't fully articulate why. That's useful — it means I don't need to justify the role's existence, just fill it.

## The Equity Question

**Opening offer: 5%**

**What 5% could be worth:** Genuinely impossible to calculate now. 5% of a small consultancy that stays local is one number. 5% of a company that lands Aramco and scales across the Gulf is a very different number. The range spans tens of thousands to millions depending on trajectory.

**What the role actually justifies:**

- Pure technical contributor in a team of 5: 2–3%
- Technical domain owner: 3–5%
- Technical cofounder bridging product, client, and engineering: 7–15%

The bridge role described is closer to the upper range. 10% is defensible if I come in with a specific high-value contribution — the observability angle is exactly that kind of thing.

**Just the two of us, clean slate:** 25–35% would be the fair range. He holds more because his network is doing the heavy lifting on go-to-market. But below 25% would start feeling like a senior hire rather than a cofounder.

**In the current team context:** The cap table question matters more than the percentage number. If the existing 5 have equity, the pool is already divided. If they're contractors or employees, the cofounder math mostly holds.

**Walk-away number:** Below 7% given this role and this moment feels like contributor pricing rather than cofounder pricing.

---

## The Play

**Don't negotiate now.** He's evaluating me, not just recruiting. Showing up with sharp questions about the product and the gaps is what leadership looks like — it's not the same as raising the equity conversation before establishing value.

**Join the weekly meetings first.** Each meeting is due diligence. I'm learning what's actually been built, who the 5 are, how he operates, whether the client relationships are as warm as they sound. I'm evaluating him as much as he's evaluating me.

**Let him write the contract.** His opening offer reveals how he's thinking about this. The contract mechanics (vesting, anti-dilution, IP assignment) tell me whether he's done this seriously before — more than the percentage number itself.

**Before signing — one conversation.** Not aggressive. Something like: *"I see myself in the bridge role between the technical team and the product, not just as an additional engineer. Does the 5% reflect that, or is there room to discuss?"* His answer tells me everything about how this partnership will work.

**What to protect:**

- My existing FinRisk/thesis work must not transfer to this venture in the IP assignment
- Check Access Group employment contract for IP clauses — understand where the lines are
- Anything built for LGN is theirs; what I've already built is mine

**In the meetings:** Show up prepared. Bring the observability angle concretely in the first or second session. Contribute ideas, don't just receive information. Let him keep seeing the same person he saw in the thesis meeting.

## Longer Arc

The immediate opportunity is clear. But the more important frame is what this enables downstream.

Financial security from this — if it works — is what buys independence to work on Malaysian and ASEAN AI governance problems that don't have obvious commercial returns. The people who end up shaping policy rarely do it from a position of fƒinancial stress.

The governance learning plan already written maps directly to this: build technical credibility (thesis done), gain institutional understanding and Gulf exposure (LGN), then work on the systemic problems from a position of independence.

The risk is letting the money, if it comes, become the reason to keep doing the money work past the point it's serving the bigger goal. Worth naming that now while the intention is clear.

This is the access layer. The systemic work comes after.
