## Issue Raised

Participant recruitment for the original 16-participant, 4-mode, Latin-square within-subjects study has stalled. One LinkedIn post with system walkthrough yielded 2 responses, 1 registered participant who went unresponsive. With the May 11th deadline, running the full study design is not realistic.

Two options were proposed:

1. **Expert evaluation (2-4 participants)** - structured expert reviews supplemented by quantitative retrieval evaluation, reframed as design science with expert validation.
2. **Downscoped qualitative study (5-8 participants)** - simplified protocol (2 modes, 30-40 min sessions), broader recruitment pool including UoB studentsπ, thematic analysis.

---

## Supervisor Recommendation

Dr. Kapetanios recommends **Option 2: Downscoped qualitative comparative study**.

### Rationale

- The existing interim report already frames the project as a human-subjects evaluation with mixed methods. A qualitative study is a natural adaptation that requires minimal reframing.
- The expert-only evaluation route would change the dissertation story more significantly. The research questions are user-experience questions (trust, perceived control, quality in use) which fit direct participant sessions better than expert review alone.
- Expert evaluation can serve as a secondary complement but should not replace the participant study.

### Recommended Design

| Parameter                       | Original                                | Revised                                                     |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Participants**          | 16 (finance professionals)              | 5-8 (professionals + advanced finance students)             |
| **Modes**                 | 4 (Baseline, HITL-R, HITL-G, HITL-Full) | 2 (Baseline vs HITL-Full)                                   |
| **Session length**        | 75-90 minutes                           | 30-40 minutes                                               |
| **Counterbalancing**      | Latin-square across tickers and modes   | Alternated condition order across participants              |
| **Quantitative analysis** | Repeated-measures ANOVA                 | Descriptive statistics only (medians, means, distributions) |
| **Qualitative analysis**  | Supplementary thematic analysis         | Primary thematic analysis                                   |
| **Expert review**         | Not planned                             | Optional secondary enhancement (2-3 experts)                |

### Mode Selection

**Baseline vs HITL-Full** provides the clearest contrast between no user oversight and maximum user oversight. This is the easiest comparison to defend in a dissertation under time pressure.

### Revised Framing

> This study presents a qualitative evaluation of a human-in-the-loop RAG system for financial risk summarisation, focusing on how differing levels of user oversight shape perceived trust, control, and usefulness.

### Pivot Justification (for dissertation)

> Owing to recruitment constraints and the time-bounded nature of the project, the evaluation was refocused from a fully counterbalanced within-subjects study to a smaller-scale qualitative comparative study. This revised design preserved the core aim of examining how human-in-the-loop controls influence trust, perceived control, and perceived usefulness, while adopting a methodology proportionate to the achievable sample size.

- **3.1 Research Design** - Small-scale qualitative comparative evaluation

- **3.8 Complementary System Evaluation** - Quantitative retrieval evaluation independent of participant sessions to contextualise user perceptions
- **3.9 Ethical Considerations** - Within existing ethics approval scope
- **3.10 Limitations of the Revised Design** - Acknowledges reduced breadth, no inferential claims, exploratory rather than confirmatory
